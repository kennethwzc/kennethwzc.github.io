The problem

Hiring for admin is a losing game

The work never stops, but the people doing it keep changing.

  • They quit, and you start again

    Every resignation means another job ad, more interviews and weeks of training before things run smoothly again.

  • A full salary for copy and paste

    Much of the role is moving data between spreadsheets, invoices and apps: paid hours for work a system can do.

  • It all lives in one person's head

    When they're on leave or gone for good, nobody else knows how the books, invoices or bookings actually work.

  • Mistakes you find at month-end

    Manual entry means typos, missed invoices and late payment chases, usually spotted when they're costly to fix.

FAQ

Common questions

Something not covered here? Send an email.

Can this really replace an admin hire?

For the repetitive part of the job, yes: data entry, invoices, bookkeeping, scheduling and reports. Anything that needs judgment gets flagged to a person, so you only handle the exceptions.

What does it cost?

The audit is a fixed fee of USD 500. Builds are quoted after the audit, once we know the scope. Optional ongoing support is USD 200 a month. Every quote says how much time the build should save you.

Do we need to be technical?

No. We build around the tools you already use, like your accounting software, website platform, spreadsheets and email. We train you and your team, and we stay on hand if anything breaks.

What happens to our data?

Your data stays in your own accounts wherever possible. We only use AI providers whose business terms exclude training on your data, and we list every provider and data flow in the design before anything is built.

Who owns what you build?

You do. Everything runs in your accounts, you get full admin access and documentation, and nothing depends on us to keep running.

How long does it take?

The audit takes 1 week and the build takes another week, so your first workflow can be live within 2 weeks.

What kinds of businesses do you work with?

Small and medium businesses in any industry. If your team does the same tasks by hand every week, there is usually something worth automating.
