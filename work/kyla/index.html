The challenge

Running a gallery and a wine bar means two businesses' worth of admin: artworks, sales, payments and expenses. Accounting alone took 5 hours a week of day-to-day bookkeeping, plus 3 hours at month-end.

What we built

Every workflow feeds the next

The bank statement feeds the ledger and the CRM, every sale updates the stock, and the stock keeps the website current, so nothing is typed twice. Scroll to follow a month of admin through the system.

  1. Step 1

    Get the bank statement

    It starts with the business bank statement: every payment in and out of the account.

  2. Step 2

    Extract the information

    Each transaction is read off the statement automatically: the date, the amount, and who paid or was paid.

  3. Step 3

    Categorise expenses

    Outgoing payments are sorted into expense categories and recorded in the ledger, with no manual tagging.

  4. Step 4

    Match payments to customers

    Money coming in is matched to the right customer in the CRM, so it's clear who has paid and who still owes.

  5. Step 5

    Post sales and debts

    Sales are posted to the ledger, unpaid balances are tracked as amounts owed, and each artist's 50% share goes into accounts payable.

  6. Step 6

    Update stock on every purchase

    When a customer makes a purchase, the CRM updates the stock in the inventory.

  7. Step 7

    Keep the website live

    The website updates itself from the inventory, with no manual edits.

  8. All connected

    One sale updates everything

    Every workflow feeds the next: the payment is matched to the customer, the sale is posted to the ledger, the stock goes down and the website updates itself. Nobody types anything twice.

The result

Beyond the hours saved, KYLA can now track the business in detail: which payments are still outstanding, how each artist is performing, and every expense, all in one place.
